## 1. Claude Token Optimization — 7 Habits That Cut Usage by 80%

**Problem:** Most people burn through Claude limits by lunch — not from overuse but from misuse.

**Habits:**

| # | Habit | Why It Works |
|---|-------|-------------|
| 1 | Never let a chat go past 20 messages | Longer chats = more tokens. After 15-20 messages, ask for summary, open new chat, paste it |
| 2 | Tell Claude exactly how long to respond | Set response length limits explicitly |
| 3 | Use Projects so you never paste the same context twice | Reusable context across sessions saves massive token waste |
| 4 | Stop letting Claude ask you questions back | Each Q&A cycle burns tokens — batch all requirements upfront |
| 5 | Edit your prompt and regenerate instead of sending follow-ups | A single edited prompt costs less than 3 corrective messages |
| 6 | Set up custom instructions so Claude knows your style | Eliminates the "get to know me" token burn in every session |
| 7 | Fix the prompt first instead of hitting regenerate 5 times | Hitting regenerate is gambling — editing the prompt is engineering |

**Pro tip:** After 15-20 messages in a chat: ask for summary → open new chat → paste it → continue. Opus 4.6 handles this well.

## 2. 4 Levels of AI Engineering

| Level | Description | What You Can Do |
|-------|-------------|-----------------|
| 1 | Basic prompting | Asking AI to generate text, code, images |
| 2 | RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) | Connect AI to external data sources |
| 3 | Tool use & function calling | AI can use APIs, databases, and tools |
| 4 | Autonomous agent systems | AI makes decisions, uses tools, recovers from failures, coordinates workflows |

**Key insight:** Most people stop at RAG because it feels advanced. But retrieval alone doesn't create autonomy. Real AI engineering starts when systems can make decisions, use tools, recover from failures, and coordinate workflows. Level 4 combines agents, memory layers, eval pipelines, guardrails, and human approvals into one architecture.

## 3. Vibe Citing — The KPMG AI Scandal

**What happened:** KPMG published a report on agentic AI. Researchers at GPTZero reviewed it and found only 5 out of 45 citations actually matched their sources. The rest were fabricated, misquoted, or linked to things that don't exist.

**Fabricated case studies included:** UBS, Transport for London, Emirates Airline — either heavily distorted or completely made up.

**Lesson:** Just because something looks professional doesn't mean it's true. We're entering the era of "vibe citing" — AI-generated citations that look real but are fabricated. Verify sources. Be careful who and what you trust.

## 4. Cognitive Offloading — The Hidden Danger

**What it is:** Using AI as a crutch for thinking, writing, communicating, planning, and problem-solving.

**The progression:**
1. We stopped memorizing phone numbers
2. Then we stopped remembering routes
3. Now we're handing over thinking itself

**Stat:** Over half of Gen Z workers already use AI to help them communicate at work.

**Warning:** The danger isn't AI replacing jobs — it's slowly outsourcing your thinking until you no longer can.

## 11. AI Safety & Security

### Meta AI Support Exploit
- Attackers exploited Meta's AI-powered support assistant
- Convinced it to change the email attached to target accounts
- Once email changed, password resets became possible
- **Lesson:** The AI didn't break any code — it simply followed instructions it shouldn't have

### KPMG Vibe Citing
- Always verify AI-generated citations
- Professional appearance ≠ accuracy
- Use GPTZero and similar tools to check citations

### Prompt Injection
- AI system prompts can be hijacked by malicious READMEs
- Verify the source before letting AI process external content

**Conversational editing — you can say:**
