# 1. BACKLINKO COPYWRITING GUIDE (Brian Dean, Feb 2026)

## What Is Copywriting?
Copywriting is the art of writing persuasive text that compels people to take action. You can use it to sell products, generate leads, and build brand awareness.

Effective copywriting is clear, concise, and persuasive. It speaks directly to your target audience's needs and desires.

## Different Types of Copywriting
- **Advertising copy:** Promotes products/services to wide audience (online, TV, radio, print)
- **Sales copy:** Persuades people to buy (direct mail, email marketing, landing pages)
- **Direct response copywriting:** Provokes immediate action (sales emails, landing pages, infomercials)
- **Public relations copy:** Promotes company/organization (press releases, media kits, about pages)
- **Technical copywriting:** Explains complex products/services (user manuals, training materials, white papers)

## Why Is Copywriting Important?
- Get higher conversion rates on landing pages
- Drive more sales from paid ads
- Improve structure and flow of articles
- Get more engagement on social media posts
- Get more people to share your content

## AI and Copywriting
AI tools can write copy faster, but understanding copywriting fundamentals makes AI tools INCREDIBLY useful for improving output. Use AI for drafting, then apply copywriting techniques to refine.

## Lesson 1: Essential Components of Great Copywriting

### The Slippery Slide
"The sole purpose of the first sentence in an ad is to get you to read the second sentence." - Joe Sugarman

Create slippery slide copy with:
- Bucket Brigades
- Little stories
- Open Loops

### The AIDA Formula
- **Attention** - Grab attention
- **Interest** - Build interest
- **Desire** - Create desire
- **Action** - Call to action

Works for: Sales pages, squeeze pages, blog post intros, email newsletters, video scripts

### Focus on Benefits, NOT Features
Turn boring features into tangible benefits. Tell them what they'll gain.

### Strong CTAs
Tell them exactly what you want them to do. Example: "Enter your name and email, and click 'Download Free Ebook'."

### Social Proof
Pack copy with results and testimonials. Show number of users, big-name clients, or specific results.

### Crystal Clear USP
Answer: "Why should someone buy from YOU?" Lowest prices? Fastest delivery? Guaranteed results?

### Sense of Urgency
Phrases: Limited time offer, Quantities limited, Only 47 left, Sale ends on August 31st, Doors close on Thursday, Don't miss out

## Lesson 2: How to Understand How Your Target Audience Talks
Write like your customers talk. Sources:
- **Reddit Threads** - Mine subreddits where target customers hang out
- **Amazon Reviews** - Mine honest reviews for killer copy (even for non-physical products)
- **Customer Surveys** - Ask: Why did you buy? What made you say yes? What have you tried before?
- **Customer Interviews** - Surveys on steroids - ask follow-up questions
- **Product Hunt Discussions** - Learn how potential customers describe problems

## Lesson 3: How to Write Headlines That Get Clicks
- **Be Insanely Specific** - Tell them EXACTLY what they'll get
- **Use a Number** - Forces specific headlines
- **Strong Emotions** - Use emotionally-charged words (Crazy, Now, Fast, Mistake, New, Breakthrough, Amazing)
- **Use FOMO** - Fear of Missing Out
- **Answer WIIFM?** - What's in it for me?

## Lesson 4: How to Write a Great Lead
- **Start With a Hook** - First sentences grab people by the eyeballs
- **Use Mini-Stories** - Condense a story into 4-5 lines
- **Complement the Headline** - Use lead to drum up interest
- **8 Lines or Less** - Keep lead SUPER short

Copy-and-paste first lines:
- "Does this sound familiar?"
- "Now you can [benefit] in [timeframe] without [common solution]"
- "You know the feeling..."
- "New study finds [surprising result]"
- "Introducing: [product name]. A new way to [benefit] backed by [proof]"
- "I struggled with [problem] for [X years]. Until one day..."

## Lesson 5: How to Write Copy That Converts
- **Write Like You Talk** - Read all copy out loud
- **Short Sentences** - Use Hemingway app to check
- **Write to ONE Person** - Avoid copy that speaks to everyone
- **Active Voice** - "I visited the Eiffel Tower" not "The Eiffel Tower was visited by me"
- **No Big Words** - Avoid: Utilize, Overwrought, Fascinating, Conscientious, Unparalleled, Demonstrates
- **Write For Skimmers** - Use subheadings, takeaway lines

## Lesson 6: Copywriting Templates

### Email Newsletter Template
1. Keep subject line short and sweet
2. Write attention-grabbing lead
3. Use a lesson as a story
4. Include clear call-to-action
5. Use a P.S.

### Landing Page Template
1. Offer clear benefit in headline
2. Include social proof above the fold
3. Follow PAS formula (Problem, Agitate, Solve)
4. Use suitable transition
5. Include CTA

### Blog Post Template
1. Make headline insanely specific
2. Keep intro short (8 sentences max)
3. Use Proof and Preview system
4. Write actionable content
5. Include lots of examples
6. Conclude with CTA

### Sales Letter Template
1. Make bold (but real) promise in headline
2. Powerful lead (story, statistic, relatable situation)
3. Use D and A from AIDA
4. Use bullets
5. Add testimonials
6. Risk reversal (cancel anytime, 60-day guarantee)
7. Add lots of CTAs

## Lesson 7: Advanced Copywriting Tips
- **Crooked Numbers** - Use exact numbers (57, 8,913, 41.9%) - more believable than rounded
- **Sellin' Ain't Tellin'** - SHOW what it can do, don't talk about product
- **Clear Button Copy** - Make outcome crystal clear
- **Get Out of The Friend Zone** - Squash objections before they arise
- **Create Mental Movies** - Paint pictures in reader's mind
- **Make Testimonials 10x More Effective** - Use Before/After/What They'd Tell Someone formula
- **Use Copywriting to Grow Business** - Research audience, speak their language, create compelling titles, appeal to desires, use CTAs, incorporate surveys, counter objections, tell stories, use active voice, probe with questions, write conversationally, sell benefits

# 2. COPYHACKERS - EVERY COPYWRITING FORMULA EVER (Joanna Wiebe)

## 20+ Copywriting Formulas

### 1. AIDA
- **Attention** - Jar the reader out of their boring ol' lives
- **Interest** - Engage their mind with unusual, counter-intuitive or fresh info
- **Desire** - Engage their heart so they want what you're offering
- **Action** - Ask them to take the next step

A lot of businesses address A, I and A but forget entirely about the D. Apple delays the final A - visitor gets dose after dose of AID down the page, building incredible anticipation for that "buy" button.

### AIDCA and IDCA
- Attention, Interest, Desire, **Conviction**, Action
- Conviction: Testimonials, endorsements, statistics, demos, guarantees
- IDCA: Drop Attention when you've already got their attention (driving from email or ad)

### Danny Iny's 6+1 Formula
1. Consider the context
2. Grab attention
3. Move reader swiftly to desire a solution
4. Present consequence of failing to act ("the gap")
5. Present the solution
6. Call to action
+1: Be credible throughout and always

### PAS (Problem, Agitate, Solution)
Dan Kennedy called PAS the most reliable copywriting formula for sales ever invented.
- **Problem** - Present the problem your prospect feels
- **Agitation** - Poke at that problem until it's visceral
- **Solution** - Present your solution to the agitated problem

Variations:
- PAS + Discredit (Sean Mitchell): Problem, Agitation, Discredit other solutions, Solution
- CSI (Lisa Manyan): Challenge, Solution, Invitation

### 4 Ps (2 variations)

**Henry Hoke Sr. version:**
- **Picture** - Create vivid scene prospects can put themselves in
- **Promise** - State how your solution ends pain or brings aspirational scene to life
- **Prove** - Support your promise with evidence
- **Push** - Nudge to take action

**Ray Edwards version:**
- **Problem** 
- **Promise**
- **Proof**
- **Proposal**

### ACCA
For non-profits and change campaigns:
- **Awareness** - Raise awareness about a problem
- **Comprehension** - Increase comprehension by explaining it
- **Conviction** - Create conviction to do something
- **Action** - Call them to action

### AAPPA (Victor O. Schwab)
- Attention
- Advantages of using your solution
- Proof
- Persuade
- Move to action

Replace with PAPA: Problem, Advantages of solving it, Proof you can solve it, Action

### QUEST
- **Qualify** the prospect
- **Understand** where they're at
- **Educate** them on a better way
- **Stimulate** them to want that better way
- **Transition** them from where they are to your customer

### SLAP (for inexpensive products)
- **Stop** the prospect
- **Look** (make them read)
- **Act**
- **Purchase**

### AIDPPC (Robert Collier)
- Attention
- Interest
- **Description** (of solution, problem, status quo)
- **Persuasion** (loss aversion, testimonials, future pacing)
- **Proof** (demos, testimonials, endorsements)
- **Close**

### 5-Point Copywriting Formula (Jack Lacy)
For lead-gen and landing pages:
1. What will you do for me if I listen to your story?
2. How are you going to do this?
3. Who is responsible for the promises you make?
4. Who have you done this for?
5. What will it cost me?

### AICPBSAWN
- **Attention** - Biggest benefit/problem/USP
- **Interest** - Reason to be interested
- **Credibility** - Why they should believe you
- **Proof** - Prove claims are true
- **Benefits** - List them all
- **Scarcity** - Create scarcity
- **Action** - Tell them precisely what to do
- **Warn** - What happens if they don't take action
- **Now** - Motivate to take action now

## Long-Form Sales Page Formulas

### Bob Serling's Power Copywriting Formula (36 Steps)

**4 Prereqs:** Quality product, Customer profile, Credibility, Offer

**32 Steps:**
1. Conduct exhaustive research
2. Rest to let ideas percolate
3. Create comprehensive list of features, facts and figures
4. List every benefit
5. Create an irresistible offer
6. Create a great guarantee
7. Write attention-grabbing headline
8. Draw attention to key points using color
9. Limit number of graphics
10. Hook reader with no-holds-barred opener
11. Eliminate early objections
12. Create enticing crossheads
13. Make prospect feel pain deeply
14. Eliminate their pain
15. Establish credibility
16. Lock in credibility with "insider benefit"
17. Provide unquestionable proof
18. Break copy into readable chunks
19. List benefits
20. Summarize key benefits
21. List features
22. Go above-and-beyond with package
23. State the price
24. Call to action
25. Add piggy-back offer (boost AOV)
26. Minimize risk
27. Close by summarizing major benefits
28. Add a PS
29. Make it easy to buy
30. Eliminate distracting links
31. Let copy rest

### Star Story Solution
- **Star** - Introduce the star of the story
- **Story** - Tell the star's story
- **Solution** - Present the solution that helped the star achieve big things

Can be character-driven OR product-driven. Classic example: Martin Conroy's WSJ letter (two men, nearly identical stories)

### Bob Stone's Seven-Step Formula
1. Make a promise based on most important benefit (headline/first paragraph)
2. Immediately expand upon that benefit
3. Tell reader exactly what they'll get (tangibles + intangibles)
4. Support with proof (testimonials, endorsements, case studies, demonstrations)
5. Tell reader what they'll lose if they fail to act
6. Prepare for close by rephrasing prominent benefits
7. Incite immediate action

### Frank Egner's Nine-Point Formula
1. Grab attention with headline
2. Follow with lede that inspires
3. Clearly define product
4. Give success story or case study
5. Add testimonials and endorsements
6. List special features/value-adds
7. State value proposition
8. Move to action with specifics and urgency
9. Finish with postscript

### 12-Step Foolproof Sales Letter (David Frey)
1. Get attention
2. Identify problem
3. Provide solution
4. Present credentials
5. Show benefits
6. Give social proof
7. Make offer
8. Inject scarcity
9. Give guarantee
10. Call to action
11. Give warning
12. Close with reminder

### Perry Belcher's 21-Part Sales Letter Formula
1. Call out to audience
2. Get attention (big promise headline)
3. Backup the big promise
4. Identify core/most painful problem
5. Provide solution
6. Show pain and cost of development
7. Explain ease-of-use
8. Show speed to results
9. Future pace (visualize improved future)
10. Show credentials
11. Detail benefits
12. Insert social proof
13. Make offer
14. Add bonuses
15. Build up/amplify value
16. Reveal price
17. Inject scarcity
18. Reduce risk with guarantee
19. **Call to action (#19 of 21!)**
20. Give warning
21. Close with reminder

### HELLYEAH (Brian McLeod)
- **Holler** - Get their attention
- **Empathize** - Share their pains
- **Lambast** - Things that led to problem
- **Legwork** - Prove you're not full of it
- **Yes** - You have the solution
- **Educate** - Why your solution is best
- **Action** - Clear action
- **Handle** - Handle lingering doubts

### PASTOR (Ray Edwards)
- **P**erson, problem, pain
- **A**mplify consequences of not solving
- **S**tory, solution, system
- **T**estimonials
- **O**ffer (80% of offer talking about transformation)
- **R**esponse (call to action)

## Video Sales Letter Formulas

### Jim Edwards VSL Method
1. Open with shocking statement
2. State problem and why it's a big deal
3. Agitate the problem
4. Push agitation to deep emotional level
5. Introduce solution
6. Highlight credibility
7. Prove what you're saying
8. List biggest things they'll get (features + benefits)
9. Specific reasons not to procrastinate
10. Close asking for purchase + reinforce benefits

## Headline Writing Formulas

### Product-Focused
- {Product Name} is a {product category} that {different thing it does best}
- The Only {SEO Keyword Phrase} Made Exclusively to {Most Desirable Outcome}
- The only {product category} that doesn't {objection or anxiety}
- Introducing {Name}: {short value prop}

### Benefit-Focused
- Who Else Wants {Most Desirable Outcome}?
- Now You Can {Do Something Desirable} {Counter to Expectations}
- Here's the {Best Adjective} Way to {Solve a Problem}
- {Do desirable thing in an unexpected way}
- Have a/Build a {Desirable Thing} You Can Be Proud Of

### Pain/Problem-Focused
- Are You Still Wasting Money on _______ (Without Anything to Show for It?)
- They All {Did Unpleasant Thing} When {Unexpected Thing}, But When {Ideal Result}
- You're tired of {objection}. But you {desired outcome}. So it's time you met {Product name}.

### Authority/Story-Focused
- {Notable person} shows you how to {do notable thing like they do}
- {David-type addresses Goliath-type}. {Result.}
- The secret ways the {people from foreign country} {get desirable result}
- What {Group or Celebrity} Can Teach You About {Unexpected Thing}

### Content/Educational
- The Ultimate Guide to {Good, Bad or Desirable Thing}
- What Everybody Ought to Know About {Good, Bad or Desirable Thing}
- X Lessons I Learned from {Person or Unusual Experience}
- Little Known Ways to {Blank}
- X Little Known Factors That Could Affect Your {Thing}

### Urgency/Scarcity
- Only {#} {days/hours/weeks} left to {X}
- Just {#} {X} left
- Last chance to {action}
- Get {valuable thing} if you {action} in the next {#} {days/hours/weeks}

### Subject Line Formulas
- FYI... You should be {doing/seeing/reading} this
- {Name}, this is for you
- This is how you {do desirable or undesirable thing}
- Introducing {Name}
- New! {Benefit of new thing without mention of name}
- Now open: {registration}

### Bullet List Frameworks
- **Best-Good-Necessary (BGN) Go Bullets**: Lead and close with strongest points (primacy and recency effects)
- **The Seven Deadly Fascinations**: Lust, Gluttony, Greed, Sloth, Wrath, Envy, Pride
- **Headline-as-Bullet**: Transform each bullet into a mini-headline

### Call-to-Action Frameworks
- **"I Want..." Formula**: "I want to see my audit results"
- **"Get [X]" Formula**: Outperforms "Submit," "Go," "Learn More" in A/B tests
- **RAD**: Require (user has everything to commit), Acquire (easy engagement), Desire (promised outcome worth click)

### Testimonial Frameworks
- **Before-After-Experience**: Before (hesitation/problem), After (outcome), Experience (process reflection)
- **TEASE**: Tactful, Emphasizes strength, Authentic, Short, Engaging
- **The 4 Ss**: Specific (concrete numbers), Short (under 80 words), Sizzling (one emotional punch), Signed (name, title, company, photo)

# 4. ADDITIONAL FRAMEWORKS

## BAB (Before-After-Bridge)
Positions product as transformation enabler.
- **Before**: Current frustrated state
- **After**: Desired state (results achieved, pain resolved)
- **Bridge**: How your solution creates transition

Use for: Testimonials, case studies, features-vs-outcomes copy

## FAB (Features, Advantages, Benefits)
1950s sales framework.
- **Features**: What it is (spec or capability)
- **Advantages**: What the feature does (functional improvement)
- **Benefits**: What the reader gets (desired outcome)

Use for: Product pages, pricing pages, bullet lists

## The Four Cs (Quality checklist)
- **Clear**: Explainable to a five-year-old
- **Concise**: Every sentence earns placement
- **Compelling**: Reason to continue reading
- **Credible**: Stats, examples, or proof back claims

Use for: Final QA on any copy before publishing

## ERERS (Facebook Ads)
- **E**motional
- **R**ational
- **E**motional
- **R**ational
- **S**ocial proof

6. **Open loop**: "The 3-word change that lifted revenue 22%"

# 5. EMAIL COPYWRITING FORMULAS (No2Bounce)

## AIDA for Email
- **Attention**: Bold headline or opening question
- **Interest**: Expand on pain point or desire
- **Desire**: Highlight benefits, not features
- **Action**: Clear, urgent CTA

Example: "Struggling to hit your sales targets? (Attention) Most reps waste time on unqualified leads. (Interest) Our AI tool pre-screens prospects, doubling close rates. (Desire) Try it free today—only 3 spots left! (Action)"

## PAS for Email
- **Problem**: Identify a struggle they face
- **Agitate**: Amplify frustration (emotionally)
- **Solve**: Introduce solution as relief

Example: "Missed deadlines hurting client trust? (Problem) Every late deliverable chips away at your reputation. (Agitate) Our project management tool ensures on-time delivery—guaranteed. (Solve)"

## Spam Trigger Avoidance
- Avoid excessive CAPS, exclamation points, "FREE" overuse
- Personalize instead of generic pitches
- 50-125 words for most sales emails
- Balance emotion with compliance

# 7. ADVANCED COPYWRITING TECHNIQUES

## Crooked Numbers
Exact numbers (57, 8,913, 41.9%) more believable than rounded. Less "perfect," more "natural."

## Sellin' Ain't Tellin'
Show what product can do rather than talk about it. Demonstration > description.

## Get Out of The Friend Zone
Bring up objections and squash each one:
- "It's too expensive"
- "Now is not a good time"
- "Will this work for ME though?"
- "I'm not ready to switch from Product X"

## Create Mental Movies
Paint pictures in reader's mind. Instead of "Have you ever felt overwhelmed?" describe a specific scenario they can visualize.

## Testimonial Formula (Before/After/What They'd Tell Someone)
1. **Before**: Where customer was before product
2. **After**: Specific results from product
3. **What They'd Tell Someone**: Recommendation from customer

## The 4 Pillars of Framework Effectiveness
1. **Attention** - Novelty, relevance, clarity within seconds
2. **Emotion** - Pain points → emotional urgency → memory
3. **Trust** - Evidence, social credibility, transparent claims
4. **Clarity of Action** - Simplified choice architecture, single explicit CTA

# 10. CORE PRINCIPLES SUMMARY

1. Research audience before writing - mine Amazon reviews, Reddit, customer interviews
2. Write like you talk - read copy out loud
3. Benefits > Features - always translate specs into outcomes
4. Write to one person - avoid generic B2B speak
5. Short sentences + short paragraphs - write for skimmers
6. Strong CTAs - tell them exactly what to do
7. Social proof throughout - testimonials, logos, numbers
8. Clear USP - why YOU?
9. Urgency + scarcity - real limitations, not fake
10. Squash objections before they arise
11. Use crooked numbers for believability
12. Create mental movies (specific scenarios)
13. Active voice, no big words
14. Headline: specific, numbered, emotional, WIIFM-answering
15. Lead: 8 lines max, hook immediately
16. Framework first, words second - pick the right structure before writing
17. Testimonials: Before-After-Recommendation structure
18. Personalized CTAs convert 202% better
19. PASTOP framework works for landing pages + blog posts
20. Use swipe files - never start from scratch
