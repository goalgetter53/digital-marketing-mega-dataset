# 3. SOCIAL MEDIA STRATEGY FRAMEWORK

## Foundation: Define Brand Identity

### Brand Positioning Statement
Answer three questions:
1. What do you do?
2. Who do you do it for?
3. Why should they care?

### Audience Personas
Go beyond basic demographics. Map:
- Pain points
- Aspirations
- Content consumption habits
- Platforms they spend time on

### Voice and Tone Guidelines
"We sound like [X], not [Y]." Create a simple voice chart to prevent brand dilution as content operation scales.

## Strategy: Plan Before You Create

### Content Pillars
Identify 3-5 recurring themes that align with both your expertise and your audience's interests.
Example (marketing SaaS): Industry Insights, Product Tips, Customer Stories, Team Culture, Thought Leadership

### Platform Strategy
Each platform has its own algorithm, culture, and content format preferences. Do not repurpose the same content identically across platforms - adapt the message to fit the medium.

### Competitive Analysis
- Study 5-10 competitors and adjacent brands
- What content formats get the most engagement?
- What topics do they avoid?
- Where are the gaps?

### Goal Setting with SMART Framework
- Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound
- Businesses that set measurable goals are 377% more likely to report success

## Goal Types
- Brand awareness (reach, impressions, follower growth)
- Engagement (likes, comments, shares, saves)
- Lead generation (form submissions, newsletter signups)
- Sales and conversions (tracked through UTM links or platform analytics)
- Customer support and retention (response rates, satisfaction scores)
- Community building

## Choosing the Right Platforms
Being on every platform is a mistake. Focus on 2-3 platforms where your target audience is most active.

### Facebook Best For
- Businesses targeting older demographics
- Community building through Groups
- E-commerce with Facebook Shop
- Local businesses
- B2C brands with visual content

### Instagram Best For
- Visual brands (fashion, beauty, travel, food)
- B2C brands targeting 18-44
- Influencer marketing
- E-commerce with Instagram Shopping

### TikTok Best For
- Brands targeting Gen Z and younger millennials
- Products visually demonstrable
- Discovery-oriented buying journeys
- Entertainment and educational content

### LinkedIn Best For
- B2B companies
- Professional services
- Thought leadership and credibility building
- Recruitment and employer branding
- Industry networking

### YouTube Best For
- Educational and how-to content
- Product reviews and comparisons
- Long-form content
- Brands with expertise to share
- SEO-driven content strategy

### X (Twitter) Best For
- Real-time news and conversation
- Customer support
- Brand voice and personality
- Industry thought leadership
- Media and publishing

### Pinterest Best For
- E-commerce (fashion, home, food, DIY)
- Brands with strong visual content
- Long-term content discovery
- Female-skewed audiences

### Text Content

- Polls and interactive content

# 7. INFLUENCER MARKETING

## Micro vs Macro Influencers
- Micro-influencers (1K-100K followers): Higher engagement, more authentic, lower cost
- Macro-influencers (100K+): Broader reach, higher cost, less engagement
- Nano-influencers (1K-10K): Highest engagement, most niche, most affordable

## Best Practices
- Align influencer audience with target demographic
- Focus on engagement rate over follower count
- Use UGC from influencers across all channels
- Provide creative freedom within brand guidelines
- Track with unique discount codes and affiliate links
- Long-term partnerships > one-off posts

# 8. ANALYTICS & MEASUREMENT

## Vanity Metrics vs Business Metrics

### Vanity Metrics (Less Important)
- Likes
- Followers/fans
- Impressions
- Reach (without context)

### Business Metrics (Important)
- Conversion rate
- Click-through rate (CTR)
- Cost per acquisition (CPA)
- Return on ad spend (ROAS)
- Customer lifetime value (CLV)
- Engagement rate (meaningful: comments, shares, saves)
- Lead generation (form fills, signups)
- Sales revenue attributed to social
- Customer retention/churn

## Tools for Measurement
- Google Analytics 4 (GA4) - traffic and conversion tracking
- Native platform analytics (Instagram Insights, Facebook Analytics, LinkedIn Analytics, TikTok Analytics, YouTube Studio)
- Social media management tools (Hootsuite, Buffer, Sprout Social, Later)
- UTM parameters for cross-channel attribution
- CRM integration for lead tracking
- A/B testing for content optimization

## Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)
- 84% of CMOs view ROI as their primary metric
- Shift from volume-based to value-based metrics
- Measure: conversion rates, customer acquisition cost, revenue attribution

# 11. 2026 TRENDS SUMMARY

## Key Trends
1. **AI in Social Media** - Content creation, optimization, analytics, customer service
2. **Video-First Distribution** - Short-form video is mandatory across all platforms
3. **Social Commerce** - In-app purchasing becoming standard
4. **Privacy-First Targeting** - First-party data essential
5. **Community-Led Growth** - Groups, niche communities, authentic engagement
6. **Creator Economy** - Micro-influencers and UGC drive results
7. **Value-Based Metrics** - ROI and business outcomes over vanity metrics
8. **Multi-Channel Convergence** - Social, search, and email blurring together
9. **Live Shopping** - Real-time purchasing during live streams
10. **Ephemeral Content** - Stories and temporary content for urgency and authenticity

## Strategy Principles for 2026
- Treat social as an operating channel, not a side task
- Align the right platform, message, and publishing rhythm to one clear business goal
- Adapt content to each platform's native format and culture
- Maintain consistent brand voice across platforms, calibrated to each audience
- "Atomize" core content into platform-specific pieces
- Build real relationships through conversations and community
- Use data to drive decisions, not gut feelings
- Balance organic engagement with paid amplification
